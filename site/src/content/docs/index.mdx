## Why

Some work ends in an answer: whether to take a major release, or how
to settle what a meeting left open. Asked inside a session busy with
something else, such a question gets a passing answer that nobody
decides and nothing records. sounding gives each question a session, a
worktree and an end of its own, and you decide the answer.

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="One question, one place" icon="seti:git">
    `sounding open` writes the question and its sources into a worktree
    on `sounding/<name>`. The session may write prototype code there to
    test an answer, and nothing lands from that branch.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Three exits" icon="right-arrow">
    The session concludes, offers a finding beyond its question to
    millpond as a capture, or asks you a question on a card and waits.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Kept to its question" icon="warning">
    A stop check keeps the session from ending with no conclusion and no
    note for the next round. A timebox ends each round, and the session
    concludes with what it knows.
  </Card>
  <Card title="You decide" icon="approve-check">
    Accept settles the question, and the work the conclusion proposes
    becomes items of its own. Go on starts another round with your
    notes. Drop records why and removes the worktree.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## Where it stands

sounding is designed and not built. Its design lives in
[agentplot/blueprints](https://github.com/agentplot/blueprints) with
the design of every tool around it, and its statement comes next. The
[roadmap](/sounding/roadmap/) says what gets built in what order.
